·Foreword

From SkyFlag to 3phor

This game began life in 2026 under the working title SkyFlag. The world, the pieces, the geometry, and the spirit of play have not changed; the brand has. The game you hold is now 3phor — the inaugural game of the Caelum Chronicles universe, of which the Kaleo Edition is the first chapter.

Everything in the rulebook v19.1 (Cross-Board Rule, Edition 1.1) is preserved in v20. What's been added comes from a year of playing the game across solo, 2P hot-seat, and online formats — additions that support the core geometry rather than alter it.

·Kaleo

A world above a world that failed

Long ago, when the planet below gave way to salt, dust, and ruined basins, the Aetheri lifted what remained of civilization into a three-layer arcology. The lowest layer — the Terran — holds the reservoirs, farms, workshops, and crowded human districts. Above it, the Meridian carries charged air, transit architecture, and the old routing systems. At the summit rests the Empyrean — the quiet upper shell where the Aetheri left their highest instruments.

At the centre of the Empyrean, and only there, waits the Caelum Nexus. The lower layers have no such central shrine. What they have, instead, are corners.

Each clan placed three dormant claim-seals in the corners of its own territory on each layer — proofs of presence left for the Aetheri to witness. Defend your seals; the opponent's seals are your targets. Only by crossing into the opposing clan's territory and neutralizing all three of its seals may a Captain earn the right to approach the Nexus. The Aetheri did not trust survival to strength alone; they built Kaleo as a system of proofs, and the proof demanded is reach.

The Aetheri did not build three layers. They built three answers to the same question.

The Two Clans

For seven generations, Kaleo was one people. The schism came during a crisis whose name the archives still record — a disease that tore through the lower districts while the council debated what to do. One faction argued for immediate escalation: open the Aetheri archives, petition the Nexus. The other argued for containment: preserve what remained, refuse panic, work with the tools already at hand.

The containment faction won the argument by one vote. Their victory stabilized Kaleo at the cost of two hundred thousand Terran residents who might have been saved. The escalation faction did not forget. Over four generations they consolidated into the Grey Ravens. The faction that had won became the White Stags. Each clan drew its name and totem from the temperament that had defined its position in that ancient debate.

Six generations later, both clans still claim duty. Both claim restraint. Both claim they are acting for Kaleo. Both are correct. Neither is complete. The contest that decides Kaleo's next age is the raid on the opposing clan's three seals, and the answer of the Nexus.

1Overview

3phor is a two-player tactical race played across a three-layer arcology. Each player commands four pieces — a Captain, a Soldier, a Rover, and a Pilot — and pursues the opposing clan's three claim-flags, one on each layer of the board. Flags may be captured in any order. Once all three of the opponent's flags are claimed, your Captain must land on the Caelum Nexus at Space(3,3) to win.

Pieces move and capture by geometry alone. There are no special abilities, no cards, no dice. Everything the game offers comes from the interaction of four movement profiles with the architecture of three stacked boards and the four vertical transit points — the Lifts — that connect them.

The objective

Each player must capture the opponent's three flags, one on each layer of the arcology. Capture all three — in any order — then land your Captain on the Nexus at Space(3,3) to win.

Board coordinates

All three layers share the same coordinate system. A cell is identified as Layer(row, col). Row 0 is the top edge; row 5 is the bottom. Col 0 is the left edge; col 5 is the right.

Layer nameIn-world nameNotable feature
GroundTerranStarting layer — pieces deploy here.
SkyMeridianMiddle transit layer. Most contested phase.

3The Four Pieces

All tactical power comes from movement, capture geometry, and positioning. Pieces use the same movement on every layer. The summary table below is followed by a detailed card for each piece.

Captain ♚ King-move: 1 sq in any of 8 directions. Lands on opponent's cell. Win-capable. Captures flags & lands on Space(3,3) to finish.

Soldier promotion

When a Soldier reaches the far row on the Ground layer (row 5 for P1; row 0 for P2), it promotes immediately to a Captain. A promoted Captain has full Captain powers: it may capture flags and land on Space(3,3) to finish the game.

Flag-capture progress is shared across both Captains — you do not need to capture all three flags with the same Captain. Players may therefore field up to two Captains simultaneously.

Pilot jump details

A Pilot at A may jump to C if there is exactly one piece (friend or enemy) at B, where A → B → C is a straight diagonal of 2 squares. If C holds an enemy piece, both that piece and the jumped piece at B are captured.

If C is empty, the move is legal — the Pilot still passes over the jumped piece but only the jumped piece is captured.

A Pilot cannot jump over more than one piece, and cannot end its move on a square occupied by a friendly piece.

4Turn Sequence

Each turn proceeds through the following steps in order. You must take every action available within your activation budget — you cannot pass if a legal action exists.

StepWhat you do
1 · Deploy (optional) Place 1 piece from hand onto your starting cell on the Ground layer. Costs 1 activation. A newly deployed piece cannot move on the same activation.
2 · Activate Take up to 2 actions with deployed pieces. Each action is one of: move a piece, capture, step up or down a Lift. You must act if any legal action is available.
3 · Flag check If one of your Captains (original or promoted) just landed on a cell containing an opponent's flag, capture it immediately. Remove the flag marker.
4 · Nexus check If all three of your opponent's flags have been captured and one of your Captains just landed on Space(3,3), you win immediately.
5 · Pass initiative The other player now takes their turn.
Activation summary

Standard: 2 activations per turn for both players.

Lift use: move to a Lift cell (1 activation on one turn), then step up or down one layer (1 activation on the next turn). The Lift step itself cannot capture.

Flag capture is automatic at step 3 — it does not consume an activation, and it grants no bonus activation.

5The Lifts

Lifts connect the three layers. They always occupy the same (row, col) on each layer: (1,1) · (1,4) · (4,1) · (4,4). These four positions form a square ring inside the board, each exactly one king-move from a flag corner on the Ground layer.

Using a Lift — two turns

Using a Lift takes two separate activations on two separate turns:

  • Turn A: move your piece onto the Lift cell on its current layer (1 activation).
  • Turn B: step your piece up or down one layer via that Lift (1 activation).

A piece cannot arrive at a Lift and step through it in the same activation. Exception: if a piece ends a turn already on a Lift cell, it may step up or down on its very next activation.

Lift occupation rule

A piece on a Lift cell on one layer blocks the Lift step from the adjacent layer. The blocking piece does not need to be on the same Lift cell as the ascending piece — it needs to be at the same (row, col) on the layer above or below.

The blocked Lift step cannot capture the blocking piece. To clear a blocked Lift, remove the blocking piece through normal play on its own layer.

Strategy: parking a defender on the Space Lift above your own flag (P1: Space(1,1); P2: Space(4,4)) simultaneously guards the approach AND blocks that Lift from Sky, denying the opponent an ascent.

Lift independence: no two Lifts are within king-move of each other. A piece on one Lift cell cannot threaten any other Lift. There is no cluster fortress — each Lift must be defended individually through positioning.

6The Flags & the Nexus

Each player captures the opponent's three flags, placed in the opponent's corners. Landing on your own flag cell does nothing — your flag stays in place until the opponent captures it. Flags may be captured in any order — Ground, Sky, and Space are fully independent objectives. Once all three of the opponent's flags are captured, your Captain must reach the Nexus at Space(3,3) to win.

Flag capture

A Captain (original or promoted) lands on an opponent's flag cell → the flag is captured instantly. Remove the marker. A friendly or enemy piece on the flag cell does not block capture — a Captain may always land on and capture an opponent's flag regardless of who else occupies it.

Flag capture does not grant a bonus activation. The turn continues with its normal budget.

After all three opponent flags are captured, your Captain must reach Space(3,3) to win.

The Aetheri Echo Point

Each player's Ground and Space flags share the same (row, col) — the Aetheri Echo Point. Continuity of position across layers was the proof the Aetheri required. The Sky flag sits at the diagonally opposite corner, enforcing a genuine horizontal traverse on the central layer.

7Win Conditions

ConditionHow it triggers
Win by Nexus (primary) A Captain on your side captures all three of your opponent's flags — Ground, Sky, and Space, in any order — and then lands on the Nexus cell at Space(3,3). Either the original Captain or a promoted Captain may trigger this win. Flags may be captured across both Captains collectively.
Win by Elimination Your opponent has no Captain on the board and none in hand, and no Soldier remaining that could promote to a Captain. All Captain-capable pieces have been permanently captured.
Win by Resignation NEW Your opponent resigns the game from the in-game toolbar. The win is recorded as a resignation in the stats.
Win by Time-out NEW If a time control is in effect and your opponent's clock reaches zero, you win immediately. Time controls are optional and default to off.
Draw by Agreement NEW Either player may offer a draw at any time; if accepted, the game ends with no winner.
Turn-limit Draw 180 turns without a win. Tiebreak, in order: (1) flags captured; (2) pieces remaining; (3) opponent pieces captured; (4) highest layer occupied; (5) Captain Chebyshev distance to Space(3,3).

Competitive Note

3phor contains a minor structural asymmetry in its spatial geometry. Specifically, Space(3,3) is not equidistant from the Player 1 (P1) and Player 2 (P2) starting positions when evaluated through optimal Captain movement paths. Under efficient play, P1 can reach this key space in 18 actions, whereas P2 requires 19. This one-action differential introduces a slight first-player advantage, analogous to the initiative observed for White in Chess. To mitigate this imbalance in competitive play, matches are structured as a best-of-three series, with players alternating starting positions between games. This format ensures that each player has equal exposure to both positional advantages over the course of the match.

8Game Phases

The three-flag system with a Nexus finish creates four natural phases of play. Flags may be pursued in any order, but the geometry rewards a sequential path.

PhaseLayerWhat happens
1 · Ground Race Ground Both Captains on Ground racing across the board to capture the opposing Ground Flag. Soldiers deploy during play. Rovers and Pilots may ascend to Sky or Space immediately. Early Lift skirmishes determine approach control. ~3–4 rounds.
2 · Sky Crossing Sky Sky transit is the hardest leg: the target Sky Flag is diagonally opposite from the Lift nearest your Ground approach (four king-moves). Both Captains must cross the full board width in Sky. Most contested phase. ~4–5 rounds.
3 · Space Flag Space After Sky, the Captain ascends to Space. With echoed corners, the target Space Flag sits at the same position as the target Ground Flag — immediate recognition, but the opponent may already be contesting the layer. ~2–3 rounds under competition.
4 · Nexus Finish Space With all three flags captured, the Captain must converge on Space(3,3). The Nexus is central — 2 or 3 king-moves from any Space Flag — and either Captain (original or promoted) can finish. 2–3 activations minimum.

9Play Modes NEW IN v20

3phor is the same game in every mode. These mode descriptions document how it's actually played on the digital editions (3phor.com on web and iOS); the rules above govern all of them.

Solo vs AI

Single-player against a computer opponent. The AI uses an iterative-deepening minimax search with alpha-beta pruning, transposition table, killer-move + history heuristic move ordering, a piece-square table evaluator, and a small opening book. Four difficulty levels:

DifficultySearch depthBest for
Easy 2 plyFirst-time players learning the geometry.
Medium 3 plyOnce you've won a few Easy games.
Hard 4 plyDefault for confident players.
Expert · Plus6 plySterner opponent. Requires the Plus subscription.

Difficulty is set in Settings → AI difficulty. The choice persists across sessions.

Two-Player Hot-Seat

Two humans sharing one device. Pass after each turn. No clock by default; enable a clock from Settings → Clock for a timed game. No account required.

Online Multiplayer

Two humans on separate devices, real-time over the network. Both players sign in (guest, email, Apple, or Google) and either create a room or join one by code. Sessions sync via Supabase realtime; push notifications fire on your turn if the app isn't focused.

  • Ratings. Every completed online game updates both players' ELO ratings. K-factor 24.
  • Friends. Save opponents' nicknames; see who's online and challenge directly.
  • Tournaments. Round-robin and bracketed events. Free events open to all; entry-fee events require the Tournament Entry entitlement.

Daily Puzzle

One shared tactical position per day. The same starting position is served to every player worldwide; solve as efficiently as you can. A briefing screen presents the objective and a short rules recap before the position is revealed (the chess.com / Lichess "I'm ready" pattern).

Time Controls

Optional. Default off. When a clock is in effect, each player has the chosen budget for the entire game — there is no per-move increment. If your clock reaches zero, you lose by Time-out.

OptionPer sideBest for
Off — Untimed casual play.
5 minutes5 min Blitz — fast, decisive.
10 minutes10 minRapid — most online matches.
30 minutes30 minClassical — thoughtful play.

Resignation & Draw Offers

Either player may resign at any time from the in-game toolbar. Either may also offer a draw; the opponent accepts or declines. A resignation is recorded as a loss for the resigner; an accepted draw counts as a draw for both. In solo play, the AI's "willingness" to accept a draw depends on the position evaluation — if the AI believes it is winning, it will decline.
